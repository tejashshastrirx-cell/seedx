TEJASH Slides 1–4

1 · Hook0:25

Walk to centre. Don't touch the clicker for 3 seconds. Look at the judges.

Imagine a farmer in Sehore. He grows good tomatoes. He works for months. And on the day he sells, most of that value disappears before the tomatoes ever reach your kitchen.

We're Tejash and Shaurya, and we built SEED X, because a farmer's harvest should not lose value before it reaches the buyer.

2 · The value gap is real0:35

This isn't a feeling. The Reserve Bank of India studied it. When you pay one rupee for tomatoes, the farmer is estimated to get about 33 paise. Onions, about 36. Potatoes, about 37.

Pause. Then: "Roughly two-thirds of what we pay never reaches the person who grew it."

It goes through traders, transport, storage and retail. Every hand-off adds cost. That's exactly the problem in today's challenge.

Point to the chain on the right as you name each step.

3 · It's a coordination problem0:30

Why does this keep happening? Not because farmers can't sell. It's because nobody coordinates.

The farmer can't see enough buyers. The hostel or restaurant can't find small local lots. Three nearby farms send three separate small trips. And every day tomatoes wait, they lose value.

"Middlemen exist because they coordinate. So we asked: what if software did the coordination, and the farmer kept the value?"

4 · One software workflow0:35

So SEED X is one simple flow. The farmer lists his harvest. Buyers post what they need. SEED X checks the crop, grade, quantity, location and timing. Then two things make it special: ClusterCart, which decides how to deliver, and NetBack, which shows what the farmer really takes home. And every order is tracked till delivery.

It's completely software. No new hardware, no machines to install. A farmer only needs the phone he already has.

TEJASH Slides 5–8

5 · ClusterCart: the wow0:45

Here's our special feature. Three farms have 80, 60 and 100 kilos. Three buyers need 70, 90 and 80. Can one truck do the job instead of six trips? ClusterCart checks crop, grade, quantity, distance, freshness, time windows and transport cost. Then it decides.

Now the part we're proudest of. We ran it on our live app. And it said no.

Pause. "Pooling would have cost 18.7% more, so it recommended separate trips, and explained exactly why."

Most apps always tell you to use their feature. Ours tells the truth, even when the answer is "don't pool". That's why farmers and buyers can trust its "yes".

Point at the green "Live run" box.

6 · NetBack0:35

Second question every farmer asks: "How much do I actually take home?" A ₹2,500 offer sounds good. But if the farmer pays ₹200 transport and ₹50 packing, he really gets ₹2,250.

"A higher offer is not always a better deal. NetBack shows the real one."

And the buyer sees his own number, the landed cost, separately. Both sides compare honestly. That's how better prices for farmers and fairer prices for buyers can both happen.

7 · Daily business workspace0:20

And it isn't a one-time listing site. It's the farmer's daily office: his stock, his offers, his orders, his clients. SEED X remembers who bought from him, so a one-time buyer becomes a repeat client. That's real, long-term income.

8 · Built for every farmer0:35

A solution is useless if a farmer can't use it. SEED X works in ten Indian languages, in their own scripts: Hindi, Bengali, Telugu, Marathi, Tamil, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi and English.

Big buttons, short steps, clear kilos and quintals, and it runs on low data. 98% of Indian internet users already use Indian-language content, so this is how India goes online.

And for farmers who aren't comfortable yet, our rollout plan is assisted onboarding through farmer groups and cooperatives.

Optional, if natural for you: say one line in Hindi: "Kisan apni bhasha mein, apne phone par, apna sahi daam dekh sakta hai."

SHAURYA Slides 9–10

9 · Trust is built into the transaction0:40

Thanks, Tejash. When money and a farmer's harvest are involved, "trust us" is not enough. So we didn't put a shield icon here. We put proof.

Every one of our 40 database tables has row-level security: a user can only see their own private data. Admin actions need two-factor login. Secret keys never touch the browser.

"We tried to break it ourselves. Farmer B trying to read Farmer A's private notes: denied. Two buyers grabbing the last stock at the same second: exactly one wins. The same order sent twice: no duplicate."

That's 141 database security tests, all passing, plus 14 browser tests including accessibility. We don't call it unhackable. We show you what's tested.

10 · Complement the ecosystem0:30

We also want to be honest. India already has e-NAM for electronic mandi trading, and AGMARKNET for daily mandi prices. We're not replacing them.

SEED X focuses on what happens around the farmer every day: his stock, local buyers, pooled delivery, his real net, his clients. It's a local workflow that can use public price data as a benchmark.

Technically: React and TypeScript on the front, Supabase Postgres with security rules on the back, hosted on Cloudflare and deployed automatically from GitHub. It's live right now. Let us show you.

Judge Q&A: likely questions and strong answers

"How is this different from e-NAM?"

e-NAM connects APMC mandis for electronic trading and auctions. SEED X sits around the farmer's daily work: direct local buyers like hostels and restaurants, pooled delivery decisions, net take-home and repeat clients. It can use public mandi prices as a benchmark. We complement it.

"Will farmers really use an app?"

That's why it's mobile-first, in ten Indian languages, with short steps and low data. Our rollout is assisted onboarding through farmer producer groups. We'll measure task completion with real farmers in the pilot; we're not assuming it.

"Is ClusterCart AI?"

No, and that's deliberate. It's rule-based and explainable: hard checks first, then a cost comparison. A farmer or buyer can see exactly why it said yes or no. Transport costs are estimates, and we label them that way.

"How much will farmers earn more?"

We won't promise a number we haven't measured. NetBack makes the real take-home visible, and ClusterCart cuts avoidable transport when pooling is actually cheaper. In the pilot we'll measure net proceeds per kilo against the farmer's current option.

"Is the data real?"

The app and database are live. The listings in the demo are clearly labelled demo data. Mandi prices are marked sample until we connect the government data.gov.in feed, which needs an API key.

"What about payments?"

Not built yet, on purpose. We don't want to fake payment status. Orders say payment is settled directly between the parties; a verified payment gateway is on the roadmap.

"What if two buyers accept the same stock?"

SHAURYA The database reserves stock in one locked step. We tested two simultaneous acceptances: exactly one wins and nothing is oversold. Retrying the same request returns the same order, not a duplicate.

"How do you make money?"

Free for farmers to list. A small fee on completed orders paid by buyers, and ClusterCart planning for institutional buyers. It's a proposed model we'll validate in the pilot.

"Who did what?"

TEJASH Founder and product lead: the problem, the farmer flow, ClusterCart and NetBack design. SHAURYA Co-developer and technical collaborator: architecture, security and testing. Both of us built and understand the whole system.

"What's next?"

Live government price data, native-speaker review of all ten languages, a payment partner, SMS alerts, and a one-district pilot with a farmer group.

Words to avoid

"Guaranteed", "first in India", "only platform", "100% secure", "AI-powered" (ClusterCart is rule-based), "real users" or "farmers are using it" (we only have demo accounts so far), any savings figure not on the slides.
