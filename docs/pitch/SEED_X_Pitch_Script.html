SEED X: the pitch, told as a story

Don't present software. Tell the story of one farmer, Dhruv, and one buyer, a hostel kitchen, and let SEED X solve their day in front of the judges. Plain words, one clear idea per slide, every feature in its natural place. About 6½ minutes.

The big idea in one line
"A farmer shouldn't need a middleman to find a buyer, check his real price, or send his crop. SEED X does that, on his phone, in his language."
The three questions every farmer asks
1. Who will buy my crop? → Marketplace + ClusterCart
2. How much will I really get? → NetBack
3. Can I trust this? → Security + honesty

TEJASH Act 1: the problem (slides 1–3)

Slide 1 · The hook≈ 30 s

Walk to the centre. Wait 3 seconds. Smile. Ask the judges directly.

Quick question. The last time you bought tomatoes, say ₹40 a kilo, how much of that ₹40 do you think reached the farmer who grew them?

Pause. Let them think.

Meet Dhruv. He's a tomato farmer near Bhopal. He wakes up at five, works the field for three months, and on selling day he has no idea who else would buy, what his crop is really worth, or how much he'll actually keep.

"We're Tejash and Shaurya. We built SEED X, because Dhruv's harvest should not lose its value before it reaches the buyer."

Slide 2 · The value gap≈ 35 s

Here's the answer to my question. The Reserve Bank of India studied it. For tomatoes, the farmer gets about 33 paise of every rupee you pay. Onions, about 36. Potatoes, about 37.

"So of your ₹40, Dhruv may see only around ₹13. The rest disappears on the way."

It passes through traders, transport, storage and the shop. Every hand-off takes a cut. So the farmer earns less, and you pay more. That's exactly this hackathon's challenge.

Point to each box in the chain as you say it.

Slide 3 · The real reason≈ 30 s

Why does this keep happening? Not because farmers are bad at selling. It's because nobody connects the dots.

Dhruv can't see the buyers. The hostel kitchen in Bhopal that needs tomatoes every week can't find small farmers like him. Three farms in the same village send three half-empty trips. And tomatoes don't wait. Every day they sit, they lose money.

"Middlemen win because they connect people. So we asked: what if Dhruv's phone could do the connecting, and Dhruv kept the value?"

TEJASH Act 2: the solution (slides 4–6)

Slide 4 · One simple flow≈ 40 s

That's SEED X. Let me show you Dhruv's day with it.

In the morning, Dhruv opens SEED X on his phone, in Hindi, and lists 900 kilos of tomatoes in under two minutes: crop, grade, price, photos. He even sets a minimum price, so nobody can offer him less.

His exact farm location stays private. Buyers only see his area until a deal is done.

Meanwhile the hostel kitchen posts what it needs: "300 kilos of tomatoes every week." Buyers can search the marketplace by crop, area, quantity, price and grade, and save their searches. When a buyer makes an offer, Dhruv gets a notification right away. He can accept, reject, or counter with his own price, just like bargaining at the mandi, but fair and on record.

"And it's all software. No machines, no new hardware. Just the phone Dhruv already has."

Slide 5 · ClusterCart: the smart truck≈ 45 s

Now Dhruv's biggest problem: transport. His 80 kilos alone don't fill a truck. Neither do his neighbours' 60 and 100.

ClusterCart looks at all the nearby farms and all the buyers, and asks: is it cheaper to send one shared truck, or separate trips? It checks the crop, quality, quantity, distance, freshness and timing, like a very honest transport manager.

And here's our favourite part. We ran it on our live app, and it said no.

"Sharing the truck would have cost 18.7% more, so it told the buyer: send separate trips. And it explained why."

Most apps always push their own feature. Ours tells you the truth, even when the answer is "don't". That's why you can trust it when it says "yes, share the truck and save".

Slide 6 · NetBack: the real money≈ 35 s

Second question every farmer asks: "How much will I really take home?"

Say Dhruv gets an offer of ₹2,500. Sounds good. But if he pays ₹200 for transport and ₹50 for packing, he actually keeps ₹2,250.

"A higher offer isn't always a better deal. NetBack shows Dhruv his real money before he says yes."

And the hostel sees its own number: the full cost to get the food to its kitchen. Both sides see honest numbers, so both can decide fairly. It even shows a dated mandi price nearby as a reference, so Dhruv knows if an offer is fair.

TEJASH Act 3: more than a marketplace (slides 7–8)

Slide 7 · Dhruv's business on his phone≈ 35 s

Once Dhruv accepts, the tomatoes are reserved instantly, so nobody else can buy the same crates. A transport partner is assigned, and both sides can track the order: pickup, on the way, delivered, done.

And here's what turns one sale into a business. SEED X remembers the hostel. In Dhruv's My Clients, it moves from "enquiry" to "active" to "repeat client". He can star his best buyers and keep private notes like "pays on time". The hostel has the same for its farmers in My Suppliers.

Every morning Dhruv's dashboard tells him what matters: open listings, offers waiting, orders on the way, money from completed sales.

"It's not a one-time selling app. It's Dhruv's shop, his order book and his customer diary, in one place."

And it helps beyond selling: a seed store where every badge says exactly what was checked, mandi prices with date and source, official government schemes like PM-KISAN and crop insurance, and even a space for landowners to share land.

Slide 8 · Made for every farmer≈ 35 s

But none of this matters if Dhruv can't use it. So SEED X speaks ten Indian languages in their own scripts: Hindi, Bengali, Telugu, Marathi, Tamil, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi and English. Even the crop names are translated.

Optional, in Hindi with a smile: "Kisan apni bhasha mein, apne phone par, apna sahi daam dekh sakta hai."

Big buttons. Short steps. Clear kilos and quintals. It works on an ordinary smartphone, even on slow internet. And our onboarding guide is a friendly farmer character called Dhruv, the same Dhruv from our story.

98% of Indian internet users already use content in Indian languages. That's how Bharat comes online, and that's how SEED X talks.

For farmers who need help at first, we'll onboard them through local farmer groups and cooperatives.

"Now, when it comes to money and harvests, trust is everything. Shaurya will show you why you can trust SEED X."

SHAURYA Act 4: trust (slides 9–10)

Slide 9 · Can I trust it?≈ 40 s

Thanks, Tejash. That's the third question every farmer asks: "Can I trust this?"

Think of SEED X like a bank. Every user's private data is in their own locker. Dhruv can't open the hostel's locker, and the hostel can't open his. His exact farm address only unlocks for the buyer and the driver once there's a real order.

Only verified people can trade: you must confirm your email first. And our admins need a second security code on their phone before they can do anything sensitive.

But we didn't just build locks. We tried to break them.

"We attacked our own app 141 different ways. Another farmer trying to read Dhruv's private notes: blocked. Two buyers grabbing the last crate at the same second: only one wins. Same order sent twice: no double order. Every single test passed."

We won't call it unhackable. Nobody honest does. But we can show you exactly what we tested.

Slide 10 · Why SEED X is different≈ 30 s

We're also honest about what already exists. India has e-NAM for online mandi trading, and AGMARKNET for daily mandi prices. They're great, and we're not replacing them.

But they work at the mandi level. SEED X works at Dhruv's level: his crops, his nearby buyers, his shared trucks, his real money and his regular customers, every single day. And it can use those public prices as a reference.

"They built the highway. We're building the road from Dhruv's farm to it."

And it's not a mock-up. It's built on the same kind of cloud technology big apps use: a modern web app, a secure database, hosted worldwide on Cloudflare. It's live right now, and anyone can open it. Let us show you.

Delivery tips that win hackathons

  • Talk to people, not to the slide. Look at a different judge for each sentence.
  • Pause after every big moment: "₹13", "it said no", "₹2,250", "every test passed".
  • Say "Dhruv", not "the user". The story is what judges remember.
  • Energy up at the start and at the close; calm and clear in the middle.
  • Never over-promise. Say "can help", "we'll measure", "estimated". Honesty sounds confident.
  • Practise the hand-off line until it sounds natural: "Shaurya will show you why you can trust SEED X."

Judge questions: simple answers first

"How is this different from e-NAM?"

e-NAM is the highway for mandi trading. SEED X is the road from Dhruv's farm: his direct buyers, shared trucks, real take-home money and repeat customers, every day. We complement e-NAM, not compete with it.

SEED X can use AGMARKNET / data.gov.in mandi prices as a benchmark once the government API key is connected.

"Will farmers really use an app?"

That's why it's in ten Indian languages, with big buttons, short steps and low data. And we'll start with help from farmer groups. In our pilot we'll measure whether farmers can complete tasks on their own; we won't just assume it.

"Is ClusterCart AI?"

No, and that's on purpose. It follows clear rules, so a farmer can always see why it said yes or no. No black box.

Hard checks first (crop, grade, quantity, freshness, timing, price floor), then it compares estimated transport costs. It only recommends sharing if it's at least 5% cheaper.

"How much more will farmers earn?"

We won't promise a number we haven't measured yet. What we can say: SEED X shows the farmer his real take-home and avoids wasteful trips. In the pilot we'll measure money per kilo against his current way of selling.

"Is it real or a prototype?"

It's live and working. You can open it now with the QR code. The listings you see are clearly labelled demo data, because we haven't started the pilot with real farmers yet.

"What about payments?"

Right now buyer and farmer settle payment directly, and the app clearly says so. A proper payment partner is next on our roadmap. We didn't want to fake it.

"What if two buyers want the same last crates?"

SHAURYA Only one can win. The moment an offer is accepted, the stock is locked. We tested two buyers clicking at the exact same second: one order, no overselling.

The database reserves stock in one locked step, and repeated requests return the same order (idempotency).

"How will you make money?"

Farmers list free, always. Buyers pay a small fee per completed order, and institutional buyers can pay for ClusterCart planning. We'll test this in our pilot.

"Who built what?"

TEJASH Founder and product lead: the farmer journey, ClusterCart and NetBack ideas. SHAURYA Co-developer and technical collaborator: building it, keeping it secure, testing it. We both know the whole product.

"What's next?"

Live government price data, a payment partner, SMS alerts for farmers without smartphones, native-speaker checks of all ten languages, and a one-district pilot with a farmer group.

Words to avoid

"Guaranteed", "first in India", "only platform", "100% secure", "AI-powered", "real farmers are using it" (not yet: demo accounts only), any saving or income number that isn't on the slides.
